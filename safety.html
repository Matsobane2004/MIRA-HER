Staying safe in South Africa

Taxis and public transport

  • Use busy, official ranks and stops
  • Sit near the driver or other women
  • Text someone the taxi’s registration number
  • Check MIRA Her alerts for your route

E-hailing

  • Match the plate, car and driver’s name before you get in
  • Share your trip with someone you trust
  • Sit in the back seat
  • If anything feels wrong, ask to stop somewhere busy

Walking, especially at night

  • Stick to well-lit, busy routes
  • Keep your phone out of sight
  • Walk with a friend and share your location
  • Trust your instincts and cross the road if needed

Dating and meeting new people

  • Meet in a public place the first few times
  • Tell a friend where you’ll be and with whom
  • Arrange your own transport home
  • Keep an eye on your drink

At home

  • Agree on a code word with someone you trust
  • Keep copies of your ID and documents somewhere safe
  • Save emergency numbers on speed dial
  • Know the quickest way out

Online and on your phone

  • Turn off location tags on photos and posts
  • Check who can see your profiles
  • Use a phone PIN that only you know
  • Screenshot threats and report them

Know your rights

Protection orders. Under the Domestic Violence Act you can apply for a protection order at your nearest magistrate’s court. It’s free, you don’t need a lawyer, and a clerk will help you with the forms.

After sexual assault. Thuthuzela Care Centres offer medical care, counselling and help opening a case in one place. Medicine to prevent HIV (PEP) works best within 72 hours, so go as soon as you can.

Reporting a crime. You can report at any police station, and you can ask to speak to a female officer.

It’s never your fault. What you wore, where you were or who you were with doesn’t make violence your fault.
