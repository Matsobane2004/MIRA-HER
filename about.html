About MIRA Her

A safe, anonymous space where South African women support each other, stay safe and get trusted information.

Why we exist

Too many women stay silent about what they’re going through. Some fear being judged. Some fear the person hurting them will find out. Others simply don’t know where to turn.

A 2024 Human Sciences Research Council study found that more than seven million South African women have experienced physical violence from a man in their lifetime. Behind that number are women who needed someone to talk to, a safe way home, or clear information about their bodies and their rights.

From MIRA to MIRA Her

MIRA began as an anonymous peer support Android app. For the Women Empowerment Through Technology Hackathon, our team rebuilt it as a website focused on women: no download needed, it works on any phone or computer, and it leaves less trace on a shared device.

MIRA Her brings together anonymous circles, trained peer listeners, community safety alerts, reproductive health information, a safety toolkit and mentorship for women in tech.

What we believe

Anonymity protects

When nobody knows who you are, you can be honest. We never ask for your name, email or phone number.

Kindness first

Every circle is moderated so it stays supportive, respectful and free of judgement.

Safety in every detail

Quick Exit, blurred locations and crisis support are built into the whole site, not added as an afterthought.

Access for everyone

A website instead of an app means it works on any device, even a borrowed phone with little data.

What works today, and what’s next

This is a working prototype. Accounts, circles, posts, peer listener chats, safety alerts, the journal and the moderator tools all work, with data saved in your browser. Next, we plan to:

  • Move to a secure online database so members can use MIRA Her on any device
  • Add AI-assisted moderation that flags crisis messages to a human moderator quickly
  • Partner with NGOs and campus wellness centres to train and verify peer listeners
  • Offer MIRA Her in isiZulu, Sesotho, Sepedi and other South African languages
  • Pilot it with students on our own campus
MIRA Her is peer support, not an emergency service. If you are in danger, call 10111 (or 112 from a cellphone), or see Get help now.
